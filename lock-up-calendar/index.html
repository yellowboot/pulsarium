What a lock-up is

A promise not to sell

Before an IPO, founders, employees and early investors sign lock-up agreements with the banks running the deal: no selling for a set time, usually 180 days. It keeps a flood of shares off the market in the first months.

Some unlock in steps

Newer deals often open in stages: a slice after the first earnings report, more after the next, or earlier if the stock trades well above the IPO price. Every stage is listed separately here.

An unlock is not a sale

When a lock-up ends, holders may sell; many don't. Prices sometimes weaken around large releases, but the date alone says nothing about where a stock goes next.

How this calendar is built

  1. The listUS IPOs priced since November 2025 that raised at least $1 billion before the over-allotment option.
  2. The termsRead by hand from each company's final prospectus (Form 424B4) on SEC EDGAR, plus later filings for early releases and waivers. Every card links to its source.
  3. The datesA step tied to an earnings report gets its date once the company announces when it reports; until then it shows as TBD. Dates are New York calendar days. Updated Nov 18, 2026. This page is information, not investment advice.
