How this is calculated

Growth: positive total revenue change and non-negative median operating margin change. Pressure: negative total revenue change and non-positive median operating margin change. At least 10 companies are required for both metrics. Other combinations are mixed; smaller revenue samples are limited.

Headline growth is the change in summed TTM revenue of the same issuers in both periods: (current total / prior-year total - 1) × 100. This is equivalent to weighting company growth by prior-year revenue. The median gives each company equal weight; breadth is the share with growing revenue. Totals describe the covered business sample, not stock-market returns or market-capitalization weights. Fiscal-quarter durations must be 70–125 days (including 12/16-week calendars) and corresponding quarters differ by at most 7 days; each TTM span and year-over-year end-date gap must be 350–380 days. Missing quarters are never filled with zeros. Each company pair uses the same revenue concept. Missing standalone quarters can be derived from differences between reported periods of that same concept sharing an exact start or end. Where Q4 is not reported separately, it can also be derived from an annual fact minus three contiguous quarters of the same concept.

Median change in four-quarter operating income divided by TTM revenue, in percentage points. Operating income and revenue must cover matching periods. Each metric shows its own sample size.

Figures come from 10-Q and 10-K reports on SEC EDGAR and describe reported results, not prices. This is not investment advice.

Questions

What does the industry signal mean?

Business growth means the industry's total revenue grew and its median operating margin held or improved. Under pressure means revenue fell and margins did not improve. Margin pressure means revenue grew while the median margin fell. Other combinations are mixed trends.

Where do the numbers come from?

From the financial statements companies file with the SEC (10-Q and 10-K reports). Each company counts with its latest comparable report; revenue is compared with the same period a year earlier.

How often is it updated?

Twice a day, as new reports reach SEC EDGAR. Companies report on different fiscal calendars, so some figures are a quarter older than others until their next report is filed.
